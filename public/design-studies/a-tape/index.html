Study A: Tape measure

Time is a length you can see. The talk is laid along a yellow tape, block by block, and the limit is a red line you drag. Anything past the line visibly hangs off the end.

Main screen

The real sample talk at 1:45. Drag the slider in any preview: every 5-second step shows the app's real selection result.

References

  • Stanley and Lufkin tape measures: the yellow blade and the tick hierarchy: small marks every 5s, labels every 15s.
  • Video editor timelines (CapCut, Final Cut): clips as blocks sized by length that you trim. Most teens already know this from editing videos.
  • Swiss railway clock (Hans Hilfiker): one red signal element. Here it is the limit line, the only thing in red apart from cuts.
  • Braun ET66 calculator (Dieter Rams): a quiet grey body, one colour for the control you touch, and labels that read like hardware.
  • Teenage Engineering OP-1: condensed type and instrument-style controls that still feel playful.

Motion

  • Slider drag. The red line follows the pointer 1:1. Blocks in the "Fits" lane resize with a 220ms ease-out, so you watch the talk shrink to the line.
  • Card cut. The block on the "Full talk" lane turns hatched red where it stands, and drops out of the "Fits" lane as its neighbours slide together to close the gap.
  • Card shortened. The block keeps its place and narrows from 46s to 8s while its colour cross-fades from graphite to blue.
  • Reduced motion. No resizing or sliding: blocks snap to their new size, and the chips and numbers change instantly.

Printed cue sheet

Cue cards print as ticket stubs: black number column, a 0.5mm border, and the tape bar in grey. The condensed type keeps them compact, so about 4 stubs fit on an A4 page.

Verdict

Strengths

  • The most direct picture of "too long": you see the overflow before you read anything.
  • The slider and the result are one object. Dragging is the demo.
  • Cool neutral palette, condensed type, very little decoration. Reads fast.

Weaknesses

  • Block labels truncate on short cards, and on a phone the blocks show only numbers.
  • Two lanes plus a ruler is the densest hero of the three. It needs one short explanation on first visit.
  • Dragging along a ruler on a 390px screen works, but 5-second steps are only about 10px apart.

Implementation cost: Medium

The tape is about 150 lines of layout code plus range-input styling. Results and editor reuse today's structure with new styles. Phone tuning takes the most time.
