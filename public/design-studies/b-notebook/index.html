References

  • Theatre prompt books and annotated scripts: cuts are struck through in place and the reason goes in the margin, so the whole show is still readable.
  • Highlighter-marked study notes: colour means status: yellow for "say this", pink for "drop this". Every student already uses this code.
  • Leuchtturm1917 and Muji dot-grid notebooks: calm white paper with a dot grid: structure without boxes.
  • Ruled 3×5 index cards: the red header line and blue rules make the cue cards look like the real thing, and print like it.
  • Atkinson Hyperlegible (Braille Institute): a body face built for legibility, including for low-vision readers.

Motion

  • Slider drag. The yellow fill follows the thumb. The big 1:42 counts to its new value over 150ms, and the highlight under it stretches or shrinks with the digits.
  • Card cut. A pink highlighter stroke draws left to right across the title and text (250ms), then the margin note fades in. Bringing a card back runs the stroke in reverse.
  • Card shortened. The full text dims and gets a thin strike. The short version unrolls below it as a yellow highlight sweeps across it.
  • Reduced motion. Strokes and highlights appear fully drawn, and the numbers change instantly. Colour and position still carry all the meaning.

Printed cue sheet

The cue cards are already index cards. On paper they keep the red header line, the faint rules print at 20% grey, and the highlights become grey tints. Two cards per row and about 4 per A4 page, like today.

Verdict

Strengths

  • The calmest and most readable of the three. It looks like the student's own work, not a dashboard.
  • One list instead of results plus editor, so there is less to learn and nothing appears twice.
  • Highlighter meaning needs no legend. The cue cards double as the print design.

Weaknesses

  • It merges the editor into the talk list, which changes the current two-section layout and needs new code for editing in place.
  • The slider is a strong control but less of a visual spectacle than A or C.
  • The dot grid and highlighter strokes need care so they never sit behind small text.

Implementation cost: Low to medium

Mostly CSS on today's markup. Merging results and editor into one list is the real work, and it doesn't touch the selection logic.
